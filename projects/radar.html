Overview

I built a LAFVIN LA048 Arduino Mini Radar Kit to get hands-on experience with embedded electronics. A distance sensor mounted on a servo sweeps back and forth, and the Arduino reads a distance at each angle and streams the data to my computer, where it's drawn as a live radar display.

After getting the stock kit working, I started modifying it to better understand how hardware and software work together.

How it works

  • Sensing: the sensor measures the distance to the nearest object in front of it.
  • Sweeping: a servo rotates the sensor through its range, one angle step at a time.
  • Microcontroller: the Arduino moves the servo, takes a reading at each angle and sends angle, distance pairs over USB serial.
  • Visualization: a program on the PC reads the serial data and draws detected objects on a radar-style display in real time.

Challenges

Making the structure to hold together the board, screen, and other parts was difficult for me. The precise measuring and the type of material chosen is very important and without any knowledge of materials, it was mainly a guided step in my project as I referred to what many people did online.

What I learned

  • Reading sensors and controlling servos from a microcontroller
  • Sending data from an Arduino to a PC over serial and visualizing it in real time
  • How hardware timing and software timing affect each other
